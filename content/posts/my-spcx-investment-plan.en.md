No one seriously doubts SPCX's monopoly position or growth story. What investors really worry about is whether buying at today's price can still deliver a long-term return they're happy with.

Investing is not only about fundamentals. Price matters just as much. A great asset at a great price is the real sweet spot.

But SPCX is a lot like TSLA: companies around Musk tend to trade with a steep premium, so a truly "cheap" entry almost never shows up. A large option-like value is also baked into the stock, which means violent swings are part of the package. If you don't have enough conviction in SPCX's vision — or in Musk himself — I wouldn't recommend owning it.

SPCX is already below its IPO price and more than 40% off its post-listing high. Rather than wait forever for an absolute bottom, it's reasonable to start buying slowly now. With names like this, you can always invent another reason to wait; and even if it does hit your "perfect" price, you may still lack the courage to size all the way up in one go. Given how I operate, a written plan and staged buys are the right fit.

## Size and pacing

I plan to buy 100–120 shares of SPCX — about 10% of my total portfolio — in three stages: before the Q2 print, after Q2, and after Q3.

The windows around Q2 and Q3 roughly line up with unlocks for employees and early investors, which could mean heavy supply. That is also when I want to accumulate. After December, most holders other than Musk himself should be unlocked, and the stock may settle into a calmer phase.

At a $1.5T market cap, SPCX is roughly around **115** per share, so I think prices **below 120** are acceptable for the long run. Around **$1T (~76)** would be deeply undervalued territory.

## How the three stages work

**Before the Q2 earnings**  
Buy 20–30 shares as close to the IPO area as I can. This stage is done: I bought 30 shares at an average of 130.

**After Q2 through before Q3**  
The first real unlock wave hits here. I plan to buy 30–40 shares around an average of 120 — start near 120, and if it breaks below, fill the rest in two clips. Another path is a post-earnings breakout: then I may add a little and decide later whether to finish the remaining size.

**After Q3**  
Ongoing unlocks of roughly 7% per period — DCA about 30 shares through that stretch. December brings a larger unlock; if a good opportunity shows up, I'll complete the last 10–20 shares there.

## Closing notes

That's basically my SPCX plan.

Valuing SPCX is hard. Some of what it may become has little historical precedent, so precise models only go so far. I'm still studying the name. Volatility can be extreme — I'm prepared for that, and living with TSLA should help.

I still think Musk is one of the few people alive who can produce extreme right-tail outcomes. Hold the position you sized for; the rest is being a friend of time.

This is personal thinking and a record of my sizing plan, not financial advice.
